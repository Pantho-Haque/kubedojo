<p class="lead">Start with one app in one container. Then build its image, connect it to another app, keep its data, and manage both with Compose.</p>
<div class="box idea"><div class="tag">The big idea</div>An image is a packaged recipe. A container is one running—or stopped—instance. Compose is a file describing a group of containers and how they connect.</div>
<p>No prior Docker knowledge is required. Read the main lesson first; expand <b>Common traps</b> when you are ready. Code blocks are examples until the hands-on lesson.</p>
<div class="worlds"><div><b>1 · Meet containers · lessons 1–3</b><span>Tell images and containers apart. Run, stop, start, and remove a pretend container.</span><button class="map-button" data-go="d-model">Begin here</button></div><div><b>2 · Build and share · lessons 4–7</b><span>Understand Dockerfiles, caching, build stages, tags, registries, and CPU architectures.</span><button class="map-button" data-go="d-file">Build the recipe</button></div><div><b>3 · Run reliably · lessons 8–13</b><span>Practice networking and storage; learn config, processes, health, security, and debugging.</span><button class="map-button" data-go="d-network">Connect the pieces</button></div><div><b>4 · Compose · lessons 14–18</b><span>Describe a stack, wait for readiness, change settings, recreate services, and recognize single-host limits.</span><button class="map-button" data-go="d-compose">Meet Compose</button></div><div><b>5 · Build and recover · lessons 19–22</b><span>Run a real two-service app, back up and restore it, then connect this knowledge to Kubernetes and Helm.</span><button class="map-button" data-go="d-real">Try it for real</button></div></div>
<p><b>Finish line:</b> rebuild the app from its files, diagnose a connection problem, recreate it without losing saved data, and restore a backup. Learn one or two lessons per sitting.</p>
